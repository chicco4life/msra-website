## Three ways into a topic

**Owner guides** explain a question, relevant terminology and useful information for a veterinary conversation. **Evidence reviews** compare a body of studies and disclose their search and appraisal limits. **Study analyses** interpret an individual paper; they do not turn one result into a clinical recommendation.

We use “systematic review” only when the work actually meets its declared review method, including search records, eligibility criteria, screening and appropriate appraisal. Ingesting many papers alone does not establish a systematic review.

## What our labels mean

We distinguish evidence strength, applicability and safety knowledge. A randomized study may be well designed but concern healthy research animals and a laboratory marker rather than illness recovery. A plausible mechanism, computational screen or another species' result is a research lead, not established benefit in your pet.

Our first owner articles are source-linked research editorial by **MSRA Research**. Independent veterinary review has not been recorded; we do not imply a veterinary author, clinical trial or peer review that has not occurred. If a qualified clinician reviews a page later, we will name them with their permission and record the date and scope.

## What we publish

We select articles for a distinct owner question or an original, useful interpretation. Many ingested papers remain in an evidence index; they do not each become a near-identical web page. We publish null and negative findings alongside positive ones and keep the original study date visible when discussing older work.

We link to original publications and lawful full text; we do not repost papers or illustrations without appropriate rights. AI assists discovery and drafting. Material claims and citations require a human editorial check before publication.

## Clinical care and commercial interests

The website cannot determine the cause of an individual animal's symptoms. A cat trying to urinate but passing little or no urine needs immediate veterinary care. Do not wait for an MSRA reply.

MSRA is developing nutritional products. An editorial conclusion is not evidence that an MSRA product treats disease. These guides do not contain disease-remedy checkout prompts. Topic emails, product information and research participation require separate choices.

## Corrections and updates

Use the [public correction form on GitHub](https://github.com/chicco4life/MSRA-Open-Research/issues/new), or email [li@msralab.com](mailto:li@msralab.com). Give the article URL, the claim and a source. Do not place identifiable medical records in a public issue. Urgent cases belong with a veterinarian, not this inbox.

We retain substantive corrections in the source history. Updated dates reflect an actual change. When new evidence affects a guide, we update the guide and connect it to the relevant study analysis instead of publishing many replacements.
