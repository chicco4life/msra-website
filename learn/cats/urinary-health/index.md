Urinary problems can leave you with new questions at every stage: what the signs mean, what happens after treatment, and how to make food or medicine plans work at home. These guides explain the research in plain language and help you prepare useful questions for your veterinary team.

**Is your cat repeatedly trying to pee with little or no urine? Contact a veterinary clinic immediately. If you cannot tell whether urine is coming out, say that when you call. Do not wait for a reply from MSRA.** [PDSA's emergency guidance](https://www.pdsa.org.uk/pet-help-and-advice/pet-health-hub/conditions/blocked-bladder-in-cats)

## Choose the question closest to yours

- **[My cat is straining or passing only drops.](/learn/cats/urinary-health/cat-straining-little-no-urine/)** What to do now and what to tell the clinic.
- **[My cat has come home after a blockage.](/learn/cats/urinary-health/after-urinary-blockage/)** Straining after catheter removal, changes to report and a clearer recovery plan.
- **[Is it a UTI, cystitis, FIC, crystals or stones?](/learn/cats/urinary-health/fic-flutd-recurrent-urinary-signs-record/)** What the words mean and what tests can clarify.
- **[Which food and water choices fit the diagnosis?](/learn/cats/urinary-health/wet-food-fountains-urinary-diets/)** Prescribed diets, ordinary wet food, fountains and food refusal.
- **[What are the medicines meant to do?](/learn/cats/urinary-health/medicines-supplements-evidence/)** Pain relief, repeat-blockage studies, antibiotics and supplement claims.

<a id="vet-visit-record"></a>

## Keep your observations in one place

Use the [free vet-visit record](/learn/cats/urinary-health/vet-visit-record/) for what you saw, what the clinic advised and what you want to ask. You can keep it on your phone or print it. There is no signup or upload requirement, and it must not delay urgent care.
