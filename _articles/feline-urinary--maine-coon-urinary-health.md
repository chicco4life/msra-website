A Maine Coon with urinary signs needs the same careful assessment as any other cat. A breed label cannot tell you whether an episode is cystitis, stones, infection or obstruction.

**Repeated attempts to pee with little or no urine require immediate veterinary contact.** Do not assume that a large cat is simply taking longer, or that a previous episode makes this one safe to manage at home. [Animal Medical Center’s obstruction guide](https://www.amcny.org/pet_health_library/urethral-obstruction-in-cats/) explains the emergency and the possibility of recurrence.

## Are Maine Coons especially prone to urinary blockage?

The evidence checked for this guide does not justify that claim.

A UK primary-care study reported nine incident urethral obstructions among 2,284 male Maine Coons during 2016: 0.39%, with a wide confidence interval of 0.18–0.75%. The whole male-cat cohort estimate was 0.54%. These are descriptive figures from one setting, not proof that the breed is unusually susceptible or protected. They also do not predict an individual cat’s outcome. [VetCompass cohort study, published 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8965234/).

Case reports that happen to involve Maine Coons cannot establish a breed predisposition either. To support one, researchers need an appropriate comparison population and consideration of other factors.

MSRA will use breed-specific language when it helps owners act, rather than turning “this can happen in Maine Coons” into “this is a Maine Coon disease.”

## Check whether the litter tray actually fits

A product sold as “large” may still be too cramped for your cat. AAFP/ISFM house-soiling guidance recommends a rectangular tray about 1.5 times the cat’s length from nose to tail base. That is a useful starting point for a larger-bodied cat; observe whether your cat can turn, position comfortably and enter without difficulty. [House-soiling guideline](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1098612X14539092).

Measure the usable inside space, particularly for covered or automatic boxes. The exterior dimensions do not necessarily describe the space your cat has. If you are considering an alternative container, think about a stable base, comfortable entry and surfaces that can be cleaned.

Fit is only one consideration. Record the location, cleanliness, litter and whether another pet interrupts access. New accidents still deserve medical assessment rather than being treated only as a tray problem.

## Separate frame size from body condition

Your cat’s weight in kilograms does not, by itself, tell you whether that individual is overweight. Ask your veterinary team to record weight alongside a body-condition assessment and, when appropriate, muscle condition. The [WSAVA cat body-condition tool](https://wsava.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Cat-Body-Condition-Scoring-2017.pdf) provides a consistent framework.

Bring the actual food amounts and treats to that discussion. Avoid comparing your cat with a social-media “ideal Maine Coon weight,” or starting a restrictive plan without knowing the appropriate goal.

## Check access in a shared home

Watch who can reach each tray, bowl and resting place comfortably. Staring, guarding a doorway or another cat leaving when a companion arrives may be more informative than whether there are obvious fights. The [2024 AAFP intercat-tension guidance](https://catvets.com/resource/2024-intercat-tension-guidelines/) describes why subtle conflict can be missed.

A useful exercise is to draw your home and mark resources and narrow routes. Add notes about what you have actually seen. Take that sketch to your vet if urinary signs recur.

## Choose a plan for the cat, not just the breed

Ask your vet what the current diagnosis is, whether dietary changes are needed and what follow-up will measure. Provide comfortable water choices; the [iCatCare drinking guide](https://icatcare.org/resources/cat-carer-guide-encouraging-your-cat-to-drink.pdf) emphasizes individual preference and gradual changes.

Keep a short baseline record of normal toileting, food and routine. It will not predict every illness, but it gives you a clearer description when something changes.
